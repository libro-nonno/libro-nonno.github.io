One of those things you never imagine could happen to you and yet, here I am telling the story. In 2002 I was working for Cisco Systems, a company founded in 1984 in San Francisco, the city from which it takes its name and which also inspired its logo, a stylized representation of the Golden Gate Bridge. The company is a world leader in internet networking technologies and, at that time, the Italian branch employed around 700 people.

In September of that year, the annual company meeting was held to present the results and the plans for the new fiscal year. The meeting took place in Riccione. Among the various activities on the agenda, one stood out: something called “Operation Logo.” It was a team-building exercise involving all employees. The goal was ambitious: to attempt something that, if successful, would be entered into the Guinness World Records. The challenge required us to recreate the company logo using the physical participation of all employees, who would position themselves in an orderly pattern to reproduce the Cisco logo from above. The criteria for the record were the number of participants and the time within which the figure had to be completed. We had only a few minutes, though I don’t recall exactly how many. The number of people was not an issue; the real unknown was whether we could beat the clock. The logo was to be formed on the beach of Riccione, right in front of the hotel where we were staying. The area had been marked off, and within that space each person had to find and occupy the designated position. The organizers handed everyone a red or blue T-shirt depending on which part of the logo they were forming, along with an inflatable beach mat of the same colour. We were to place the mat in the sand and lie on it. Naturally, we were given precise coordinates for our assigned spot, almost like an Excel spreadsheet brought to life.

Once the shirts were on and the mats were inflated, we were ready. A notary, called in to certify the proper execution of the attempt, climbed onto a raised platform built for the occasion. Using a megaphone, he gave the final instructions and, at the sound of the siren, we all began moving briskly toward our assigned coordinates. We laid down our mats and stretched out on them as quickly as possible. After a short time, the notary, having verified that the shape was complete, stopped the stopwatch and sounded the siren to mark the end of the attempt. Then came the announcement, again through the megaphone: “Congratulations to all! You have completed the challenge! You are in the Guinness World Records!” His words were met with a long, thunderous round of applause.
