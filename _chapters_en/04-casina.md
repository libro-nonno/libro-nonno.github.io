At about thirteen years old, together with a group of friends, we decided to build a small hut — which we called the “casina” — on the bank of the Càffaro river.

I don’t exaggerate if I call it a little masterpiece of imagination, creativity and engineering skill, born from that irrepressible urge for adventure that only a group of youngsters can experience. We were a group of peers, all attending middle school: I, Fernando, Giancarlo, Gianluigi, Gianstefano, Oliviero, Osvaldo, Renzo and Silvano. I don’t remember exactly how the idea began or how the plan was drawn up, but the gang formed quickly, all eager to start building the casina. 

We conducted surveys, inspections and asked the landowners for permission. In the end we chose a clearing on the opposite bank of the Càffaro river — a spot a little distance from the water yet safe from seasonal floods. It was perfect; being reachable only by crossing the river made it even better, since only a few fishermen could get there and it fed our spirit of adventure. 

We began to assign individual and group tasks while sketching plans and listing the materials needed. Giancarlo contributed the most to the design phase — his father had a construction company and could help us source materials from job sites.

## The Construction

We worked with great enthusiasm the whole summer to bring our project to life. Little by little the casina took shape: a studio of about sixteen square meters built with beams and boards, with doors, windows, a concrete floor and a fireplace that would warm us and allow us to roast chestnuts in the cool autumn days. The furniture consisted of a wall shelf, a table and some chairs. You didn’t need the whole gang present to use the casina — anyone could go there with a friend. 

To signal our presence and be reached, we raised a white flag on a pole visible from certain points in town. The place became a much-desired gathering spot, but also the subject of gossip. Many wondered what we did there and what secrets the refuge might hide. Even the parish priest spoke about the casina in one of his sermons, portraying it as a den of sin and its regulars as sinners - without ever having spoken to us directly.

## The Cableway

Crossing the river was a huge obstacle. The place we had chosen could only be reached on foot by a long path. Transporting all the materials along that trail would have been unthinkable, so we decided to string a metal cable between the two banks. The cable was laid and secured and could be used to haul materials for the casina. 

After a few test runs the system passed inspection and we decided it could also be used to carry people. Initially we used a simple but effective system: a crossbar attached to the pulley hook that you gripped with both hands, and in a few seconds you were on the other side. What a thrill! No harness, the water flowing beneath us. Even though Gianluigi had watched many crossings, he was fearful and very hesitant to try it. 

We pressured him a lot and finally convinced him to try. Bent at the knees, he closed his eyes and — instead of letting himself glide along the cable — he gave an upward push so the pulley came off the wire and he fell straight down like a stone. Splash! We heard the loud slap of a belly-flop and burst out laughing. It was nothing serious — Gianluigi simply had to let his clothes dry. It didn’t take long before the cableway was completed, equipped with a crate sturdy enough to carry people too.

## The Carabinieri Inspection

Word about the casina had spread and everyone had an opinion: some for it, some against it — so the local marshal of the Carabinieri decided to investigate and came to visit. Four or five of us were at the casina when the marshal and two officers appeared on the opposite bank. They said 
they were there to carry out an inspection and asked us not to move — but they didn’t know how to cross the river. 

We immediately helped: one of us hopped over the stones and showed them the way, then they crossed. Once at the casina they asked to come in and inspected our things. They conducted a short inquiry: names, parentage, who frequented the casina, whether we had weapons, etc. In the end the marshal praised our construction, told us to behave well and asked to be escorted back to the other bank. 

News of the inspection spread quickly through the village and — as often happens — two factions emerged: those who believed something shady was going on and those who pointed out that nothing irregular had been found and that we were simply enterprising, adventurous kids.

## Easter Monday at the Casina

Buying a new outfit was a big event and usually happened only near major holidays like Easter and Christmas. That year my mother bought me a new suit. I wore it on Easter and was very proud. I wore it again on Easter Monday. 

Since it was a beautiful day, the group decided to have a picnic at the casina. Reaching the river, we began to cross by hopping on the stones protruding from the water. When it was my turn, on the last jump I slipped and fell into the deepest part; I emerged with my suit soaking wet. 

We lit the fireplace, I took off the suit and put it near the flames to dry quickly. It took a long time, and when it was time to go home, I put it on even though it wasn’t completely dry. The pant legs and sleeve hems had shortened by six or seven centimetres! My friends laughed a lot. I, on the other hand, was gloomy and resigned, convinced I would receive a good scolding from my parents — and that I’d have to wait another year for a new outfit.

## The Teachers’ Visit

The casina was no longer a secret. Its name circulated in town and even our teachers were curious. Some third-year teachers were so interested by our description that we invited them to visit. They gladly accepted. On the agreed day we were all excited and ready to receive such important guests. The cableway had been checked in every detail and worked perfectly. 

From the riverbank you could barely make out the casina, hidden by willows, and the teachers were impressed. We ferried them one by one in the cabin hung on the cable; though initially hesitant, no one refused the crossing — perhaps that adventurous trip made the teachers feel a bit like kids again. 

The Handicrafts/Technical Studies teacher went first, followed by the French, math and art teachers. 
The literature teacher, an older woman, declined the invitation. Once at the casina, the teachers were clearly astonished; they hadn’t expected such a well-made and well-finished structure. They asked many questions about how we had built it and how we intended to use it. 

We lit the fireplace to make tea, offered some drinks, and the visit ended amid compliments. We then escorted the teachers back to town. 
When third-year exam results were posted, the casina crew proudly found a straight TEN in Technical Application — a sweet vindication against many detractors. That TEN was the final proof of how good our project had been. Thanks, professor!

## The X ray glasses

This chapter finds its place in this book thanks only to a simple, pleasant coincidence.
On September 6, 2025, my son Filippo, my daughter-in-law Silvia, my two grandchildren Camilla and Giacomo, and I decided to hike up to the little church of San Gervasio, perched on the mountainside facing Bagolino, offering a stunning view of the village below. After taking in the panorama and enjoying the small thrill of ringing the church bell a few times, we began our walk back.

On the way, I couldn’t miss the chance to stop by and greet my friend and former classmate Giorgio who, during the summer months, moves to the beautiful farmhouse he restored, located right along the trail. We were both genuinely happy to see each other again; opportunities to meet have become rare over the years.

While chatting about this and that, we ended up talking about the “casina” and, in particular, about all the rumours that had circulated in town about “the boys from the casina.” Giorgio had never been part of our group, yet he told me he remembered that period vividly, and confirmed that the topic had been on everyone’s lips in the village.

He added that he recalled perfectly how people said that “the boys from the casina” had supposedly ordered special glasses that allowed you to see under people’s clothes. It wasn’t true. And as far as I remember, those legendary “special glasses” were never purchased. What is true is that, through the Postal Market catalogue, we had ordered snorkelling masks and a few pairs of fins to use for swimming in the river.

![img](/assets/img/09.png)

As often happens, imagination outruns reality: those simple snorkelling masks became, in the local lore, X-ray vision goggles that “the boys from the casina” used to peek under girls’ clothes! 

If even Giorgio, who was never part of the clan, still recalls the story so clearly, it means it was an important episode, one worth remembering.
Looking back, how ahead of our time were we, “the boys from the casina”? 
There was no internet, no Amazon, and yet we managed to get products delivered to our tiny hamlet tucked away in the mountains. A small miracle, and one I’m still proud of today.

Author’s note: The truth about those mythical X-ray glasses is that they were nothing more than a big commercial scam, a genuine swindle. Much to the disappointment of all the detractors of “the boys from the casina.”
